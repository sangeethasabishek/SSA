The DPDP Act Checklist Every Small Business Owner Needs

So you saw our reel, typed "DPDP" in the comments, and here you are. Good call.

Let's be honest: for years, nobody in India really cared about data privacy. We handed out our phone numbers at every billing counter, filled every Google Form, and tapped "Accept all" without reading a word. Businesses did the same with customer data: saved it everywhere, shared it with anyone who asked, and never thought twice.

That's changing. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the DPDP Act) comes fully into force on 13 May 2027. That's about six months away. If your business collects customer phone numbers, names, photos or addresses, this law is talking to you.

The good news? Getting ready isn't complicated. You don't need a legal team. You need an afternoon, a notebook, and this checklist.

"I'm just a small shop. Does this even apply to me?"

Probably, yes.

The law applies to anyone who handles people's personal data in digital form, including data written on paper and later typed into a phone or computer. There's no turnover limit and no "too small to matter" clause.

Think about your own business for a second:

  • Run a salon? You've got names, phone numbers, appointment history, maybe before-and-after photos.
  • Run a bakery or restaurant? Phone numbers on bills, delivery addresses, a WhatsApp full of order chats.
  • Run a clinic or pharmacy? Patient names, prescriptions, test reports.
  • Run a tuition centre? Children's names, ages, marks, and their parents' numbers.
  • Run a shoe shop or any retail store? Billing numbers, loyalty points, leads from your Instagram ads.

If any of that sounds familiar, the law sees you as a Data Fiduciary: the person who decides why customer data is collected. Your customer is the Data Principal. Any app or vendor that handles the data for you, such as your billing software or WhatsApp marketing tool, is a Data Processor.

Don't worry about the jargon. Here's what you actually need to do.

The 12-step DPDP checklist

We've grouped these into four parts. The first four steps take an afternoon. The rest are habits you build into how your business runs.

Part 1Know your data

  1. 1

    Find out where your customer data lives. Grab a notebook and list every place a customer's details end up. Your billing software. The old bill book. WhatsApp. Google Forms. Instagram DMs. CCTV footage. And yes, your staff's personal phones. Next to each, write what you collect, why, and who can see it. Most owners are surprised by how long this list gets.

  2. 2

    Stop collecting what you don't need. Does your billing really need a customer's date of birth and home address? If you only need a phone number, only ask for the phone number. The law says consent only covers data that's actually needed for the purpose you stated.

  3. 3

    Check your vendors. Your billing app, WhatsApp marketing tool, delivery partner and even your digital agency all touch your customer data. Each one should have a written contract with you that covers how they keep that data safe.

Part 2Get consent right

  1. 4

    Write a simple notice. This is a short, standalone message that tells customers, in plain language: what data you're collecting, why you need it, how they can withdraw consent, how they can use their rights, and how they can complain to the Data Protection Board. Put it on your billing counter, your website or your form. No fine print.

  2. 5

    Ask separately for marketing. This is the one most businesses get wrong. A phone number given for billing is not permission to send festival offers on WhatsApp. If you want to send offers, ask clearly: "Can we WhatsApp you our offers?" Make it a separate question, and never use a pre-ticked box. The customer has to actively say yes.

  3. 6

    Make "stop" as easy as "yes". If a customer agreed with one tap, they should be able to say no with one tap. Something as simple as "Reply STOP to unsubscribe" works.

  4. 7

    Don't forget your old customers. That list of 5,000 numbers you've built over the years? The law covers it too. Send your existing customers the same notice as soon as you reasonably can. You can keep using their data until they ask you to stop.

Part 3Protect it

  1. 8

    Lock down who can see what. Put passwords on your billing system and phones. Give each staff member their own login instead of one shared password. And when someone leaves, remove their access that same day. Keep regular backups so you don't lose data if something goes wrong.

  2. 9

    Keep a record of who accessed data. Use tools that log who opened or downloaded customer data, and keep those logs for at least one year. If something ever leaks, this is how you'll find out what happened.

  3. 10

    Have a plan for when things go wrong. If customer data leaks, you have to tell the affected customers without delay and report it to the Data Protection Board, with full details within 72 hours. Decide today who in your business handles this. You don't want to be figuring it out on the worst day.

Part 4Respond to your customers

  1. 11

    Give customers one person to contact. Put a name, email or WhatsApp number on your website or app where customers can ask about their data or raise a complaint. Customers can ask what data you hold, ask you to correct it, or ask you to delete it. You must reply within 90 days.

  2. 12

    Delete data when you're done with it. When a customer withdraws consent, or you no longer need their data for the reason you collected it, delete it. Ask your vendors to delete it too. The only exception is when another law requires you to keep it, such as tax records.

A special note if you work with kids

Run a tuition centre, a kids' salon, a toy shop or a summer camp? Pay extra attention here. Under this law, anyone under 18 is a child. Before you use a child's data, you need verifiable consent from a parent. And you can't track children's behaviour or target ads at them.

So what happens if you ignore all this?

Let's talk numbers, because they're big. There's no jail under this Act, only fines, and each amount below is the maximum.

If you…Fine up to
Don't keep customer data reasonably safe, and it leaks₹250 crore
Don't tell customers and the Board about a breach₹200 crore
Break the rules on children's data₹200 crore
Break any other rule in the Act₹50 crore

Now take a breath. ₹250 crore is a ceiling, not a starting point. The Data Protection Board has to consider how serious the breach was, what you did to limit the damage, and whether the fine is proportionate. So the sensible steps you take now can count in your favour.

That's the real point of this checklist. It's not about fear. It's about showing your customers, and the law, that you take their trust seriously. And honestly? Customers notice that.

Your next step

Pick one step from this list and do it this week. Most owners start with Step 1, because once you see where your customer data actually lives, everything else gets clearer.
