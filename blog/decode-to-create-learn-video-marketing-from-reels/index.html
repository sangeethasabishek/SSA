Your feed is a free masterclass

Every reel that stopped your thumb today was engineered: the hook, the cuts, the product at second 4, the CTA. Watch it as a decoder, not a viewer, and your own reactions become a reusable playbook.

One question drives everything in this blog: why did this work on me?

Why decode: viewer vs decoder

A viewer saysA decoder says
"That was fun""Face + text hook in 1s, product at 4s"
"Let's go viral""Let's reuse the hook that stopped us on Tuesday"
"Make it engaging""Hook under 1s, a cut every 2s, soft CTA"
"I don't like it""The hook lands at 2.5s; it needs to land under 1s"

The payoff: ideas from a library instead of a blank page, sharper briefs for editors, and trends spotted in week one, not week five.

Anatomy of a scroll-stop

A winning reel does five small jobs in order. If one fails, the viewer scrolls and nothing after it is seen.

Six hook types, left to right: Visual · Text · Audio · Question · Bold claim · Pattern interrupt.

  • Why is the product at second 4? The problem has just landed, so the product arrives as the answer. At second 0 it feels like an ad; at second 15 most viewers have gone.
  • Why that CTA? Soft ("link in bio if you're curious") is for cold viewers. Hard ("40% off, ends tonight") is for warm ones. Decode a CTA by asking who it was aimed at.

The decoder's question bank

Ask these in the order the viewer lives them: Stop → Stay → Feel → Save → Click → Borrow.

StageAsk yourself
StopDid it interrupt a pattern? Was the hook visual, text or audio?
StayWhat loop did it open that I needed closed? How fast were the cuts?
FeelWhy was it relatable? Did it feel like an ad or like content?
SaveWhat value made me want to come back or send it to a friend?
ClickWhen and how did the product appear? Why that CTA, that offer?
BorrowWhat format and trend is this? Which part fits my brand?

Save now, decode later

Rule: if a video makes you pause, it goes in the swipe file within 10 seconds. Decode in a weekly batch.

  1. Save to a named Instagram collection ("Hooks", "UGC ads", "Trends"); check the Meta Ad Library for ad versions.
  2. Log the link with one name pattern: Date_Platform_Brand_HookType_Format.
  3. Tag hook type, format, emotion, product-reveal second, CTA, and one line on why I saved it.
  4. Decode weekly: 60 minutes, 5 to 10 videos. That is 500+ decoded reels a year.

The SODME DECODE framework

Every video our team studies runs through six steps, so two people decoding the same reel reach the same kind of insight.

The step most people skip is O, Observe the why. Naming the psychology (curiosity gap, FOMO, social proof, relatability) is what turns a nice reel into a reusable rule.

One per video, 5 minutes. Copy it into Notion or a sheet.

PROOF  Product at __s, shown how, proof type:
CTA    Exact words, at __s, soft or hard:

Avoid: copying the video instead of the pattern · decoding only your own industry · writing "it's good" instead of how it made you feel.
